- **Result**:
  - A Scan now ends with a new step, "Hold-up Test". It sorts the Scan's Wallets into two groups using only their result on the bets before the Cut-off Date: the Before-Cut-off Passers (*Skilled (high confidence)*) and the Comparison Group (*Likely skilled* or *No evidence of skill*).
  - Each group's later bets are pooled and run through the existing Skill Test, with bets in the same Market drawn together, so Wallets that copy each other do not count as separate evidence. A Wallet needs 10 later bets to count; a group needs 30. The two results are stored with the Scan.

## 2. Planning

Epic 5 had no summary file yet, so the AI wrote one from the PRD, the architecture and the UX documents (`epic-5-context.md`). It then read the code the story builds on and drafted the spec with three questions, each with a recommendation.

1. **Where do the 1,000 lucky-streak Wallets live?**
   - (a) For the checks only: the generator makes them and the tests use them, but the offline app never loads them.
   - (b) Loaded and found by the offline Scan.
   - (c) Only a picked handful that passed, in the Scan.

   The AI recommended (a): in the Scan, about 990 of them would join the Comparison Group, and the real Wallets that Story 5.2's README result is about would be lost among 99,000 made-up bets.

   **Edvard did not answer straight away. He asked first:** "what happens to the coin-flippers that are already in the scan when the groups get made? do they even have bets after the date". The AI had not looked at that. It measured it on the offline Scan stored on his PC: the 1,000 coin-flippers have no bets after the date at all (their 100 bets run from January to April 2025). So 6 are Passers by luck, 994 are in the Comparison Group, and all 1,000 are dropped for having too few later bets. That means the app's own warning, "More Wallets were dropped in one group than in the other…", will show on the offline Scan (70% dropped among the Passers against nearly all of the Comparison Group).

   **Edvard then chose (a) and made a second decision of his own:** "the warning is kinda right tho, those coin-flippers really did stop betting before july, so i dont wanna bend the rule to hide it. just make sure 5.2 says why it shows up offline so it doesnt look like a bug". The AI wrote that into Story 5.2 in `epics.md` as a new acceptance line, so it cannot be forgotten.
2. **Helpers.** **Edvard chose review only** (AI rec), as in 4.6 and 4.7.
3. **Spec size** (over BMAD's 1,600-token guide). **Edvard kept it whole** (AI rec). The AI had said about 2,000 tokens; with his decisions written in it came to about 2,400, and the AI told him so before he approved.

Three smaller choices the AI made itself and showed him at the checkpoint: a group's "not fully read" count covers every Wallet in the group; a Scan finished before this story keeps its results but has no Hold-up result; large groups are simulated in smaller blocks that give the same numbers, so a big live Scan cannot run a laptop out of memory.

## 3. Build

The AI built it directly from the spec:
- `core/holdup.py` (new): who is in which group, the 10-bet rule, the pooled test and the dropped-share warning flag.
- `core/skilltest.py`, `core/rng.py`: the Skill Test can now be run for a group as one random unit, and draws its 10,000 Simulations in smaller blocks when a group is large.
- `data/schema.sql`, `data/store.py`: a new table for the two group results. They are written in the same step that marks the Scan finished, so a finished Scan always has them. Older database files keep their Scans.
- `services/scan.py`: the new last step of the Scan job.
- `data/synthetic.py`: the lucky-streak Wallets. The coin-flippers' files and expected results are unchanged.

**One deviation from the spec's file list:** the spec planned a new `services/holdup.py`. The AI put the new step in `services/scan.py` instead, because the two files would otherwise have had to import each other. `services/holdup.py` comes with Story 5.2's page.

**What a full offline Scan now gives** (run through the real app path, 51 seconds, the new step takes 0.3 seconds):

| Group | Wallets | Dropped | Tested on | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Before-Cut-off Passers | 10 | 7 (sharp trader and 6 coin-flippers) | 300 later bets of steady sharp, forecaster and speed trader | *Skilled (high confidence)* |
| Comparison Group | 999 | 996 (994 coin-flippers and two real Wallets with 9 and 4 later bets) | 409 later bets of 3 real Wallets | *No evidence of skill* |

The warning flag is set (70% against 99.7% dropped), as Edvard decided it should be. Story 5.2 will put the real Wallets' result in the README with its limits.

**The checks the plan asked for:**
- Steady sharp is still *Skilled (high confidence)* on its later bets.
- The 7 lucky streaks show *No evidence of skill* on theirs (700 bets). The list of 7 was found by running the real Skill Test over all 1,000, not picked, and a test proves it against the generator.
- A made-up example with 60% and 10% dropped sets the warning; equal shares do not.
- 7 Passers holding the same side of the same 40 Markets look skilled (p < 0.05) in 37 of 1,000 planted runs, under the 7% limit. Counted as 280 separate bets instead, the same runs would look skilled in 249 of 1,000: that is the false result the shared draw prevents.

The step runs the same engine and checks that each Wallet's own tests already passed. Written into AD-22, and told to Edvard.
