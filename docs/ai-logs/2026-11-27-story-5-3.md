## 3. The trial Scan (10 Wallets)

The AI started the app online and started the trial. The page's estimate was "about 10 minutes"; the AI's own guess to Edvard was 10 to 40 minutes. **Both were wrong: it took 2 hours 20 minutes.**

- The Markets step took 20 minutes: 77 of 100 Markets used, 189,086 Wallets to choose from. (The data study in October found 77 and 189,085.)
- The second Wallet on the shortlist, 0xd218e4, is very large. After 39 minutes of reading it, Polymarket answered 503 three times. The Scan stopped as designed, and what had been read for that Wallet was thrown away, because the app stores a Wallet's data whole or not at all.
- The AI pressed *Resume Scan*, which the spec's table says to do for a stop. The second attempt took about 38 minutes and went through.
- Result: no Wallet rated *Skilled (high confidence)*, so no Passers. Comparison Group: 7 Wallets, *No evidence of skill*, about 1 time in 9, on 2,072 bets.

Edvard asked a couple of times how it was going and asked to be told when it was past the large Wallet and when it was done; the AI told him both times, and told him plainly that its time guess had been wrong.

**What the trial showed about the app** (no code was changed):
- The time estimate cannot be trusted.
- One very large Wallet costs about 40 minutes.
- A single run of 503 answers loses that Wallet's work and leaves the Scan stopped until someone presses Resume.

## 4. Edvard's call: how to survive a stop overnight

The 200-Wallet Scan would run into the night. The AI gave three ways to handle a stop:

- (a) a small script, outside the repository, that presses *Resume Scan* two minutes after a stop;
- (b) pressing it by hand;
- (c) changing the app to retry by itself.

It recommended (a): (c) is new code in a story that adds none, days before the code freeze.

**Edvard:** "a, write the helper".

The AI wrote `resume_scan.py` in the data folder outside the repository. It only reads the database (read-only) and sends the same request the button sends, at most 30 times, never for a Scan that failed or was interrupted, and logs every press. The AI tried it against a throwaway database and a stand-in app first (five cases). It also told Edvard that the PC goes to sleep after two hours and gave him the command to switch that off for the night.
