## 2. Decisions

Before writing the spec, the AI checked the plan's rules against the Wallets captured during the data spike. That turned up two places where the plan didn't hold up on real data, so the AI stopped and asked.

| # | Question | AI's recommendation | Edvard's decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Which number is a bet's Entry Price? The architecture said Polymarket's `avg_price`; the PRD said "the average of the buys". They differ whenever a Wallet sells: on 0x736483, 986 of 1,388 sold bets differ, while all 1,459 bets without sells match | The average of the buy fills, because it is the price actually paid and the Exit PnL formula needs it. One line of the architecture changes | Agreed |
| 2 | Traders who "sell everything" often keep a tiny remainder (0x736483: 713 bets with under 1% left; the hand-calc Wallet 0x29306d: 3). The rule "fully sold only at exactly 0" counts each one as a bet held to the end | Treat under 1% left as sold | **Changed it**: under 1% **and** worth under $1 counts, and its money stays in Real PnL; it is only left out of the bet count |
| 3 | Follow-up: "worth" measured how? | At most $1 if it won (fewer than 1 share), because a rule using the real result would drop losing slivers and keep winning ones, making a Wallet look luckier | Agreed |
| 4 | Follow-up: if the sliver's money counts in Real PnL, it must also be in the 10,000 replays, or the comparison is uneven. Is that what he meant? | Yes, in the money and the replays, never in the bet count | Agreed |
| 5 | The spec (checkpoint 1), about 1,600 tokens | Approve and build now | "Approve and continue" |
| 6 | Subagents | Same as Story 1.1: the AI writes the code, subagents only review | Agreed |

Decisions 1 to 4 changed the plan before any code was written (commit 056c33b). The changes were the PRD Glossary (new term **Sliver**), FR-3 and the assumptions index, the architecture's AD-10 source table and AD-11, Stories 1.2 and 1.4 in the epics, and an entry in each memlog.

Choices the AI made itself, written in the spec:
- Bets in Markets that are not closed yet are outside the count, so open bets never show up as "unclear resolution".
- Share counts are rounded to 6 decimals, Polymarket's own precision. Real Wallets have 13 to 22 tokens each where bought − sold leaves float noise like 0.0000000000000036.
- Broken input rows (a trade with no token or side, an unknown row type) raise an error instead of being counted wrong without anyone noticing.

## 3. Bugs found in the AI's code

| Found by | Bug | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Blind and edge-case reviewers | Exactly 1% left became a Sliver for some sizes: 0.01 × 70 is 0.7000000000000001 in floating point. The AI's test used 90, one of the sizes that happens to work | The limit is rounded like the shares; the test now covers 35, 57, 70 and 90 |
| Test-gap reviewer | Three rules could be broken without any test failing: the 1-share limit, the token/price length check, and a trade row with an empty token | A test for each, then each reviewer's planted bug was rerun and fails now |
| The AI, rechecking its own fix | The first test for misaligned Market lists used a Market the old code already rejected for another reason, so it still didn't test the length check | Replaced with the reviewer's case (3 tokens, 2 prices) and checked again |
| Edge-case and test-gap reviewers | Smaller ones: an `assert` that disappears under `python -O`, the Market ID taken from the wrong place, an empty sum giving the integer 0 instead of 0.0 | Fixed |

The AI also planted bugs itself before the review. Six of seven failed a test. The one that didn't was removing the sort that makes the sums independent of input order. Since Python 3.12, `sum()` adds floats in a way that barely depends on order, so no realistic input can show the difference. The sort stays as a cheap guarantee, and this is written down.

In total the three reviewers raised 21 distinct findings. 9 were fixed. 8 were rejected with evidence, for example "the plan forbids removing duplicate fills (Story 2.2)" and "4,135 slightly negative fees in the captured data are all under $0.00001; Story 2.3's fee check covers them". 4 were deferred to `deferred-work.md` with what would settle them. The full table is in the spec's Review Triage Log.

A one-off run on the captured data (not committed) gave 23 bets in resolved Markets for 0x29306d, the "23 bets" named in Story 2.7. Under the new rule, that is 18 tested, 3 Slivers and 2 sold.
