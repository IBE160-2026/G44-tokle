## 2. Planning

The AI pulled `main` and made the branch. A helper compiled the Epic 4 summary (`epic-4-context.md`) from the PRD, architecture and UX documents. The AI then read the window code and the spike's capture script, and probed the real capture with throwaway scripts in its scratch folder (no network):

**Helpers.** The AI recommended helpers for the summary and the review, with the AI writing the code itself, as in earlier stories. **Edvard chose differently: helpers for the summary, the build and the review.**

**Mixed Markets.** Where should a Market go if one side was bought before the date and the other after it? The AI gave two options:

- (b) only the later Position goes in the after part, as the Glossary reads word for word.

The AI recommended (a). **Edvard chose (a).** The AI wrote it into architecture AD-7 and the memlog before any code (commit 0452c06).

**Spec size.** The spec was about 2,100 tokens against the workflow's 1,600 guideline. The AI recommended keeping it whole. **Edvard kept it whole** and said "approve and continue".

## 3. Build, pass 1

A fresh helper built the spec. All tests passed (1,228). The after part was chosen by reading activity forward from the date until 2,000 Positions had a first buy, with a new stop rule (`until`) added to both clients.

## 4. Review pass 1 and Edvard's decisions

Three reviewer helpers filed 18 findings. 12 were real, and 6 were rejected with a reason. Before deciding, the AI checked the important ones against the real capture:
- **The AI's planned rule had a hole.** "A Market counts once the Wallet buys in it" lost Markets entered by a conversion or split, with no buy. On 0x729806 that is 352 resolved Markets. They vanished from the after part uncounted, while the before part counts the same kind of Market as left out.
- **A real bug.** 3 Markets each on 0x729806 and 0xd218e4 were closed by Gamma before the date but bought only after it, and would have landed in both parts.
- **Other problems:**
  - the "left out by the cap" flag could be wrong in both directions;
  - the after part's activity read ran on to today;
  - five checks could be deleted without any test failing. The verification reviewer showed this by breaking each one on purpose.

The rule itself was at fault, so the AI asked Edvard two questions, each with a recommendation:
1. **Rank the after part by first entry?** The positions list's first-entry time matches the first buy to the second in 6,786 of 6,967 real after-part bets, and the other 181 were entered by a split or conversion. **Edvard chose first entry** (AI rec). Rejected: keeping "needs a buy" and noting the gap.
2. **Does a bet fully closed before the date make its Market mixed?** The pass-1 helper had let such Markets count, which keeps the result the same whatever the page size. **Edvard: it does not make it mixed** (AI rec). Rejected: reading every Wallet's whole closed history.

The workflow sends such a case back to planning, so the AI reverted the code and kept a copy outside the repo for reuse. It rewrote the spec, changed AD-7, AD-10, `epics.md` and the memlog, and kept a list of what pass 1 did well.

## 5. Edvard's question before approving the revised spec

Edvard asked: *"can a bet that's bought after the date end up in the before part anywhere? check the matrix against the leak row."* **It could.** The AI's revised spec put "Gamma closed before, bought after" Markets in the before part only. That broke the no-leak rule, and the leak test would not have caught it, because it only changed Markets resolved after the date.
- The AI recommended "in neither part", so the two parts mirror each other. **Edvard agreed.**
- The AI also named one small leftover: the positions list's share count is today's, so shares bought after a Market resolved can only drop a before-part bet as a share gap, never add one. **Edvard asked to note it, not change it.**

Both are in the spec, AD-7, `epics.md` and the memlog (commit 37897cc). Then Edvard said "approve and continue".
