## 1. The prompt

Edvard asked the AI to read the handoff note, pull `main` (2.7 was merged), and give its call on "the fee rate thing, the strict check throwing out fees that fit the older rate" before Epic 3 starts.

## 2. The AI's recommendation and Edvard's decision

The AI explained the problem: Polymarket's market data (Gamma) gives only a Market's fee rate *today*. Story 2.7's independent script found that all 9 fees the check threw out (0x0f4b0a 7 fees, $0.28; 0x091596 2 fees, $44.48) fit the fee formula exactly at 4%, 5% or 7%, while the Markets now list 1% or 3%. So the check was punishing Polymarket's rate changes, not bad data, and Real PnL came out slightly too good.

It gave three options with a recommendation:
1. **Accept any whole-percent rate from 1% to 10%** (recommended): still catches negative or odd fees, accepts fees that were real when paid.
2. Keep the strict check and only reword FR-4: no code, but Real PnL drifts further from the truth every time a rate changes.
3. Trust every recorded fee: simplest, but drops the check FR-4 promises.

The AI also pointed out that this reverses Edvard's own call from 21 November (Story 2.3: "the check stays strict"), so it would be logged as a reversal with the reason.

**Edvard: "yes go with option 1".**

## 6. Review

Three reviewer helpers read the diff and filed 20 findings; 6 were fixed, the rest rejected with a reason (all in the spec's triage log). The fixes:
- the reference comparison now also checks the **number of failed fees** against the reference, not just the totals (a planted strict rule now fails it: 2 vs 0);
- the window tests check that no fee fails in left-out Positions either;
- a test for a fee below the 1% floor (0.5% fails);
- clearer names in the code (`WHOLE_PERCENT_RATES`);
- the reference test's docstring says how the third helper was set up;
- the Epic 2 summary said rates were "lowered"; they change both ways.

One finding the AI rejected but Edvard should know: on very small fills, the whole-percent rates lie so close together that almost any fee up to 10% passes. The money involved is at most about $0.002 per fill, and the strict check was just as loose there.
